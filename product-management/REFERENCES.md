# References

Where to go for more, organised by the level each source serves. Every link here returned a live
page when checked on **12 October 2026**. Anything that could not be reached from a plain request
was left out rather than quoted from memory, which is why some well known names are missing.

A note on how to use this. The sources below are not a reading list to work through. They are
places to go when a specific lesson raised a question this course deliberately stopped short of
answering, and the right time to open one is when you have a decision in front of you, not before.

## Books worth the time

No links, because editions and sellers change. These are the ones whose arguments recur across
this course.

| Book | Why |
|---|---|
| *Inspired*, Marty Cagan | The clearest statement of what product teams are for |
| *Continuous Discovery Habits*, Teresa Torres | Discovery as a weekly practice rather than a phase |
| *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*, Kohavi, Tang and Xu | The definitive book on experimentation, and the source of most A/B folklore |
| *The Mom Test*, Rob Fitzpatrick | How to ask questions that cannot be answered politely |
| *Monetizing Innovation*, Ramanujam and Tacke | Pricing as a design input rather than an afterthought |
| *Good Strategy Bad Strategy*, Richard Rumelt | Why most strategy documents contain no strategy |
| *AI Engineering*, Chip Huyen | The systems view of building with models |
| *Thinking in Systems*, Donella Meadows | Why interventions produce the opposite of what was intended |

## What is deliberately not here

Certification programmes, prompt engineering courses, and anything selling a framework as a
career. The useful material in this field is written by people describing what they did, and it is
free.
