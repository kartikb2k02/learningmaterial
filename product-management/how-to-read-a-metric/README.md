# How to Read a Metric

Read this before any specific number. It prevents most of the errors the rest of the measurement
topics would otherwise walk into: the wrong denominator, the mix shift, the average that hides
two populations, and the metric nobody can attach a decision to.

| # | Lesson | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|

| 03 | Rates, Ratios and Averages | Why is the average nearly always the wrong summary? |
| 04 | Distributions, and Why You Need the Shape | What does a median hide that a percentile reveals? |
| 05 | Simpson's Paradox and Mix Shift | How can every segment improve while the total gets worse? |
| 06 | Leading, Lagging and Vanity | How do you tell a predictive number from a flattering one? |
| 07 | Windows, Cohorts and Snapshots | Why do two correct answers disagree about the same week? |
| 08 | Survivorship and Selection | Which population is actually in your numerator? |
| 09 | The North Star and Its Critics | What is a single top metric good for, and where does it mislead? |
| 10 | Guardrails and Counter Metrics | What stops a team optimising one number into a worse product? |
| 11 | Instrumentation: Getting the Number at All | What has to be true before a metric exists? |
| 12 | Worked: Two Dashboards That Disagree | One real discrepancy, diagnosed to its cause |
