# The Growth Function

Before any channel or any experiment, the function itself. Six lessons on what a growth team
actually owns, why that is a rate rather than a feature, where the team sits in an organisation,
what it is permitted to touch, which of the familiar growth practices are theatre, and what good
work looks like when a third of your experiments are supposed to fail.

| # | Lesson | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | [What a Growth Team Actually Does](01-what-a-growth-team-actually-does.md) | What are the three jobs, and why is the team's name on the arrows rather than the boxes? |
| 02 | [Growth, Marketing and Product](02-growth-marketing-and-product.md) | Where are the real lines between the three, and what breaks at each seam? |
| 03 | [Where Growth Sits](03-where-growth-sits.md) | Standalone team, embedded, or nobody: what does each arrangement actually produce? |
| 04 | [The Growth Charter](04-the-growth-charter.md) | How do you write down what this team may touch, before the first argument rather than after? |
| 05 | [What Growth Is Not](05-what-growth-is-not.md) | Which familiar practices borrow against the future, and what is the exchange rate? |
| 06 | [What Good Looks Like](06-what-good-looks-like.md) | How is this job judged when a third of well designed experiments are meant to fail? |

Read these in order. Everything later in the course assumes the three jobs in lesson 01 and the
boundary argument in lesson 02, and lesson 06 is the one that sets the standard the rest of the
course is written against.
