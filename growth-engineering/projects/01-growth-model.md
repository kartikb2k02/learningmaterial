# Project 1: A growth model for a real company

**Produces** one spreadsheet, one page of assumptions with dates, and a ranked list of three inputs
worth working on. **Takes** two days. **Assumes**
[The Growth Model](../the-growth-model/README.md).

## The brief

Pick a company whose numbers you can partly observe: your employer, a public company with filings,
or a product with a published pricing page and a visible funnel. Build the model that says how it
grows.

## What it has to contain

1. **One arithmetic statement** for the output, written as an equation rather than a sentence.
2. **Between four and seven input metrics**, each one something a team could change in a quarter.
   If you have more than seven, you have included outputs.
3. **Every cell marked** as either a measurement with a source and a date, or an assumption with the
   reasoning for it. No unmarked cells.
4. **A sensitivity table**: each input moved by ten percent, with the effect on the output, ranked.
5. **One page of what the model cannot promise**, naming the three places it would break first.
6. **One idea it kills.** Find something the company is plausibly considering and show the model
   rejecting it.

## The hard parts, which are the point

You will not be able to measure most of the inputs, and the temptation is to replace a measurement
with a plausible number and move on. Mark it as an assumption and state what it would take to
measure it. A model whose assumptions are labelled is useful; a model that looks like measurement
and is not is worse than nothing.

The second hard part is item 2. Separating inputs from outputs is the whole skill, and the test is
whether somebody could be given the number as a target without being able to game it.

## How to know you are finished

Give the spreadsheet to somebody who knows the company and ask them to find a number they disagree
with. If they cannot find one, the assumptions are not visible enough. If they disagree with more
than half, you have guessed where you should have measured.
