# Project 5: An experiment result memo for a test you did not run

**Produces** an eight part result memo and an estimate of how likely the result is to be real.
**Takes** half a day. **Assumes**
[The Experiment Machine](../the-experiment-machine/README.md).

## The brief

Find a published growth result: a conference talk, a company blog post, a case study, a conversation
on a podcast. Rewrite it as the memo the team should have written, and then estimate how much of it
to believe.

Choose one that reports a number. "We redesigned onboarding and activation rose 34 percent" is
ideal, because the gap between that sentence and a usable memo is the whole exercise.

## What it has to contain

The eight parts, in order, filled in from the source where possible and marked as unknown where not.

1. The decision, in one sentence.
2. What was changed, precisely enough that somebody could rebuild it.
3. The population: assigned or exposed, and how many.
4. The primary metric, its effect and its interval.
5. The guardrails and what they did.
6. The planned looks and the threshold used.
7. What they expected before they started.
8. What they now believe, and what would change their mind.

Then two additions of your own.

9. **The share of unknowns**, counted. Most published results leave five or six of the eight
   unstated, and counting them is the most useful thing in this project.
10. **Your estimate of how likely the result is real**, using their plausible win rate, the power
    their sample supports, and the number of looks they probably took.

## The hard parts

Item 3 is usually unknowable from the source and is the one that decides whether the number means
anything, because an effect measured on assignment is diluted by the exposed share. Write what you
would need to know.

Item 10 will produce a number that feels rude. It is not a judgement on the team; it is what the
arithmetic says about any result reported without power or looks, and the exercise is to notice how
much the missing information costs.

## How to know you are finished

You should be able to say, in one sentence, what you would ask the author if you met them. If that
sentence is "what was the sample size", go back to item 3, because there is a more specific question
available.
