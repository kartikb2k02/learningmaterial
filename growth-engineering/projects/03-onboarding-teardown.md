# Project 3: A teardown of one onboarding flow

**Produces** a step table with durations and pass rates, the single largest cliff, and one costed
recommendation. **Takes** one day. **Assumes**
[Activation and Onboarding](../activation-and-onboarding/README.md).

## The brief

Sign up for a product you have never used. Record the flow precisely, then estimate what it is
costing and what one change would be worth.

Do it twice: once as yourself, carefully, and once in a hurry on a phone. The difference between the
two is most of what the teardown finds.

## What it has to contain

1. **A step table**: every step from landing to first value, with the seconds it took you and your
   estimate of the share who get through it. State how you estimated each pass rate.
2. **The cumulative column**, and the single largest cliff identified.
3. **Drop out per minute** for every step, and the ranking by that measure next to the ranking by
   duration. Note where they disagree.
4. **A candidate activation definition** for this product, with the action, the window, and your
   estimate of its separation and coverage.
5. **The empty state assessed**: what it shows, which of the five strategies it is, and what it asks
   the user to do.
6. **One recommendation**, costed: what to change, what you expect it to be worth in points of
   activation, and the arithmetic behind that expectation.
7. **The measurement plan** for your recommendation: enrolment time at their plausible signup rate,
   and what you would read.

## The hard parts

Estimating pass rates from the outside feels unscientific and is the exercise. Use whatever you can:
how many fields, whether it leaves the product, whether it requires data you would not have to hand.
State your reasoning so somebody can disagree with a specific step rather than with the whole
estimate.

Item 7 is the one most people skip. A recommendation with no measurement plan is an opinion, and the
arithmetic in lesson 07 will often show that the change cannot be measured at that company's volume,
which is itself a finding worth writing down.

## How to know you are finished

Your recommendation should survive the question "what if you are wrong about the pass rate on step
four". If it does not, you have recommended the step you found most annoying rather than the one
costing the most.
