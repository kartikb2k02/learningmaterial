# Growth Economics

Every definition in this topic is set in
[Revenue and Efficiency](../../product-management/revenue-and-efficiency/README.md); nothing here
restates one. What these six lessons add is the arithmetic a growth lead has to do with those
definitions: which inputs are yours, why a blended number hides the decision, where the next pound
belongs, which budget argument survives, what to say when the model and the target disagree, and
when losing money per customer is a strategy.

|---|---|---|
| 01 | [Reading Unit Economics as a Growth Lead](01-reading-unit-economics-as-a-growth-lead.md) | Which numbers are yours to move, and which metric should you be measured on? |
| 02 | [Payback by Channel](02-payback-by-channel.md) | Why does a blended number hide the decision, and which test should run first? |
| 03 | [The Marginal Pound](03-the-marginal-pound.md) | Where does the next unit of spend go, and how do you measure it? |
| 04 | [Setting and Defending a Budget](04-setting-and-defending-a-budget.md) | What argument survives the finance meeting, and which constraint actually binds? |
| 05 | [Forecasting Against a Target](05-forecasting-against-a-target.md) | What do you do when the model and the target disagree? |
| 06 | [Buying Revenue at a Loss](06-buying-revenue-at-a-loss.md) | When is it a strategy, and when is it a slow failure? |
